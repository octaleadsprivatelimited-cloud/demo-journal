# Aims & Scope

## Connecting cardiovascular research with clinical practice
Singapore Journal of Cardiology provides a forum for research and discussion about cardiovascular health, disease prevention, diagnosis and treatment. It serves clinicians, researchers, educators and allied health professionals who need clearly reported evidence and thoughtful interpretation.

## Topics within scope
Contributions may address coronary disease, heart failure, rhythm disorders, structural and congenital heart disease, vascular medicine, cardiac imaging, interventional cardiology, cardiovascular surgery and rehabilitation. We also welcome relevant work on risk factors, population health, multidisciplinary care and diagnostic or therapeutic technologies.

## What makes a suitable submission
A manuscript should identify its clinical or scientific question, describe methods transparently and distinguish findings from interpretation. Case reports should offer a clear learning point; reviews should explain how evidence was selected. Findings that challenge existing practice or report negative results can be valuable when the study is sound.

Work without a meaningful cardiovascular connection may be outside scope. For an uncertain topic, send the editorial office a proposed title, short summary and article type before submission. See [Author Guidelines](/policies/author-guidelines).

# Editorial Team / Editorial Structure

## How editorial responsibilities are organised
The journal’s editorial work brings together manuscript administration, subject expertise and independent assessment. Current named appointments are listed on the [Editorial Board](/editorial-board); a contributor profile does not itself indicate an editorial appointment.

## Editorial leadership
Editors-in-chief oversee editorial standards, address escalated concerns and guide final publication decisions. A handling editor assesses a manuscript’s relevance, selects appropriate reviewers, evaluates their reports and communicates the decision to the corresponding author.

## Editorial office and reviewers
The editorial office assists with submission completeness, correspondence, files and publication preparation. Reviewers provide specialist advice on the work’s methods, interpretation and reporting. Their recommendations inform the decision; they are not a vote that automatically determines the outcome.

## Accountability
Editors must disclose interests that could affect impartial handling and transfer conflicted manuscripts to another editor. Questions about a manuscript should include its reference number and be sent through the author workspace or [contact form](/contact). Complaints about editorial conduct follow the [complaints and appeals process](/policies/complaints-appeals).

# Peer Review Process

## From submission to editorial decision
Peer review evaluates the relevance, methods, reporting and interpretation of a manuscript. Submission is not acceptance, and a favourable recommendation does not guarantee publication.

## Initial assessment
The editorial office checks that the manuscript, author information, declarations and supporting files are complete. The handling editor assesses scope, originality and ethical concerns. A paper may be returned for missing information or declined before external review when it is unsuitable for the journal.

## Specialist assessment
Suitable manuscripts are assigned to reviewers with relevant expertise. Reviewers must disclose competing interests, protect unpublished material and explain the evidence behind their recommendations. The editorial office should communicate the identity-disclosure arrangements applicable to the review before it begins; authors should not assume that identities are concealed unless instructed.

## Decisions and revisions
The editor may request changes, seek further advice, accept or reject the work. Revision requests identify the issues to address. Authors should submit a revised manuscript and a point-by-point response, explaining any requested change they have not made. Acceptance is followed by publication checks and proof preparation. An appeal must identify a specific error or procedural concern; see [Complaints & Appeals](/policies/complaints-appeals).

# Publication Ethics & Malpractice Statement

## A reliable scholarly record
Everyone involved in publication shares responsibility for honest reporting, appropriate attribution and respectful evaluation. The journal’s ethics policies apply to submissions, peer review, published articles and post-publication correspondence.

## Responsibilities
Authors must report work accurately, identify contributors, disclose relevant interests and obtain the approvals and permissions their work requires. Reviewers must preserve confidentiality and give independent, reasoned advice. Editors must assess submissions fairly and address credible concerns without allowing commercial considerations to determine scientific decisions.

## Raising a concern
Send the article title or manuscript reference, a clear explanation and relevant evidence through the [ethics contact channel](/contact?category=ethics). Avoid including patient identifiers or confidential institutional records in an initial message. The editorial office may request a secure way to review supporting information.

## Assessment and action
An allegation is not a finding of misconduct. The journal seeks an explanation from the relevant parties and may consult an institution or other appropriate body. Editorial action may include further review, rejection, a correction, an expression of concern or retraction. The response should address the reliability of the record and remain proportionate to the evidence.

# Plagiarism Policy

## Originality and attribution
A submission must clearly distinguish the authors’ own work from material obtained elsewhere. Copying wording, ideas, data, figures or structure without appropriate acknowledgement is unacceptable. Translation does not remove the requirement to cite the original source.

## Responsible use of earlier work
Use quotation marks for direct quotations and provide a citation. Paraphrasing must reflect an independent explanation rather than a few substituted words. Cite related publications from the same authors and disclose overlap with preprints, conference abstracts, theses or earlier reports. Obtain permission for material when its licence requires it.

## How concerns are evaluated
Similarity indicators, when used, are a starting point for editorial assessment rather than an automatic verdict. There is no universal percentage that establishes originality. Editors consider the location, extent and significance of overlap, including whether methods or referenced quotations account for it.

## Author response
Authors may be asked to explain overlap and supply the relevant source material. Depending on the evidence, the editor may request correction, decline the submission or consider action on an already published article. Related concerns should be reported through [Research Misconduct](/policies/research-misconduct).

# Research Misconduct Policy

## What to report
Concerns may involve fabricated or altered data, manipulated images, deceptive attribution, undisclosed duplicate publication, compromised peer review or unethical research. A disagreement about interpretation alone does not establish misconduct.

## Submit a documented concern
Use the [ethics contact form](/contact?category=ethics). Identify the article or manuscript, explain the suspected problem and point to specific passages, figures or records. Provide a contact address for follow-up. Do not obtain evidence through unauthorised access or publish personal information about the people involved.

## Editorial assessment
The editorial office records the concern and considers whether an immediate publication hold or further evidence is needed. Authors should have an opportunity to respond. Where investigation requires access to research records or supervision beyond the journal’s authority, the matter may be referred to the relevant institution or other responsible organisation.

## Outcomes
Editors act on the available evidence to protect the scholarly record. Outcomes may include no further action, requested clarification, additional review, rejection or a linked publication notice. Confidential details are shared only where relevant to assessment or required by applicable obligations. See [Corrections & Retractions](/policies/corrections-retractions).

# Authorship & Contributorship

## Who should be an author?
Authorship recognises intellectual work and carries responsibility. A named author should have made a substantial contribution to the work, participated in drafting or critical revision, approved the version to be published and accepted accountability for its integrity. Funding, supervision or administrative assistance alone is not sufficient.

## Contribution statements
Describe each author’s contribution in plain language, such as study design, data collection, analysis or manuscript revision. Acknowledge other assistance with permission. Automated tools cannot take responsibility for a publication and must not be listed as authors.

## Corresponding author
The corresponding author coordinates submission, ensures that co-authors approve the manuscript and declarations, and responds to editorial queries. Names, affiliations and contact information must be checked before publication.

## Changes and disputes
Explain any proposed addition, removal or reordering of authors and obtain written agreement from all affected authors. Editors may pause handling when an authorship dispute is unresolved. Institutions may need to determine contributions where the journal cannot verify them.

Further reading: [ICMJE guidance on authors and contributors](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/defining-the-role-of-authors-and-contributors.html).

# Conflict of Interest / Competing Interests

## Disclose interests that could influence the work
A competing interest may be financial, professional or personal. Disclosure allows readers and editors to assess the work with appropriate context; an interest does not automatically disqualify a contribution.

## Authors
Declare relevant employment, consulting, fees, grants, stock ownership, patents, paid testimony and support for the reported study. Also describe non-financial relationships or roles that could reasonably affect interpretation. Identify the funder’s role in design, analysis, writing and the decision to submit. If there are no relevant interests, state this explicitly rather than leaving the declaration blank.

## Reviewers and editors
Disclose close collaboration, institutional connections, personal relationships, direct competition or financial interests relevant to the manuscript. Do not accept an assignment when you cannot provide an impartial assessment. An editor with a relevant conflict should arrange independent handling.

## Updates
Notify the editorial office if an interest is discovered or changes during review. A material omission discovered after publication may require a published clarification. Questions about whether an interest is relevant should be raised before the editorial decision.

# Research Ethics / Ethical Oversight

## Approval or exemption must be explained
Research involving people, human material, identifiable information or animals must have the oversight appropriate to its design and setting. State the responsible review body, decision date and approval or reference number. If formal review was not required, explain the basis for exemption and who determined it.

## Protect participants
Describe measures taken to protect participants’ welfare, dignity and privacy. Additional safeguards may be necessary for people whose circumstances limit their ability to make a free decision. Consent to take part in research and consent to publish identifiable material are separate questions.

## Documentation
Editors may ask for approval letters, protocol information or an explanation of amendments. Do not upload identifiable consent forms or private participant records as public supplementary material. Contact the editorial office to arrange appropriate handling of confidential evidence.

## When concerns arise
An unexplained absence of required approval may prevent further consideration. Retrospective editorial permission cannot replace research oversight. See [Informed Consent](/policies/informed-consent) and [Human & Animal Research Ethics](/policies/human-animal-research).

# Informed Consent Policy

## Participation and publication are different permissions
Authors must explain how consent for research participation was obtained or why an authorised waiver applied. A participant’s agreement to receive care or join a study does not automatically permit publication of identifiable details.

## Identifiable case material
Obtain appropriate publication consent before submitting a case report, photograph, recording or other material through which a person might be recognised. Explain to the person that published material can be accessible online and shared by others. Where a representative gives consent, describe the basis for that authority.

## Minimise identification
Remove names, record numbers and unnecessary personal details. A covered face or altered initials alone may not prevent identification. Do not change clinically relevant facts to disguise identity; omit unnecessary details and explain relevant de-identification measures instead.

## Records and enquiries
Keep the consent documentation securely and state in the manuscript that publication consent was obtained. Do not place signed forms in public supplementary files. If consent cannot be obtained, contact the editorial office before submission; publication must not be assumed to be permissible.

# Human & Animal Research Ethics

## Research involving people
Report the ethics committee’s approval or exemption and the consent arrangements. Explain participant selection, privacy safeguards and any measures relevant to vulnerable groups. Describe the applicable ethical framework and local requirements rather than relying on a general statement that the study was ethical.

## Clinical trials
Identify the trial registry and registration number, include the registration date and explain any registration after recruitment began. Provide a protocol or link where available, disclose changes to outcomes and describe data-sharing arrangements. Prospective registration is the approach recommended by [ICMJE](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/publishing-and-editorial-issues/clinical-trial-registration.html).

## Animal studies
Identify the institutional oversight body and authorisation. Describe species, sample-size reasoning, housing, procedures, pain management and humane endpoints as relevant. Explain how the design considered replacement, reduction and refinement of animal use.

## Transparent reporting
Use reporting guidance suited to the study design and provide enough methodological detail for readers to understand the work. Editors may request supporting documentation or clarification before review or publication.

# Data Sharing & Reproducibility Policy

## Explain where the evidence can be found
Include a data availability statement describing the data supporting the findings, where they can be accessed and any conditions or restrictions. A statement should be specific enough for a reader to understand what is available; avoid a general promise that cannot be fulfilled.

## Data, code and methods
Where appropriate, deposit data and analysis code in a suitable repository and cite the persistent record. State software versions, relevant parameters and material departures from the planned analysis. Distinguish the data underlying the article from supplementary illustrations or example files.

## Protect confidentiality
Open sharing must respect consent, privacy, intellectual property and other applicable restrictions. Do not upload patient-level identifiers. For restricted data, explain the reason, the type of access available, the responsible contact and any approval required. Controlled access may be more appropriate than unrestricted publication.

## Author responsibility
Authors should retain the records needed to support their findings and cooperate with reasonable editorial enquiries. Corrections to a dataset or code repository should be versioned and explained, particularly when they affect the article’s conclusions.

# Intellectual Property / Copyright Policy

## Rights in submitted work
Authors are responsible for submitting material they have the right to publish. Submission does not, by itself, transfer copyright or establish a reuse licence. The applicable publication agreement and the rights statement on each article determine the rights granted for publication and reuse.

## Third-party material
Check the permission or licence for every borrowed figure, table, photograph, questionnaire and substantial extract. A citation acknowledges a source but does not necessarily authorise reproduction. Supply permission documentation when required and include the requested credit line.

## Reusing journal content
Consult the article’s copyright and licence statement before reproducing or adapting it. Material attributed to a third party may have different conditions from the surrounding article. If the rights statement is absent or unclear, contact the editorial office with the article details and proposed use.

## Copyright concerns
Use the [contact form](/contact) to identify the material concerned, explain your rights and provide supporting information. The journal may request clarification and consider correction, restricted access or other appropriate action after reviewing the evidence.

# Licensing Policy

## Check the licence on the individual article
A licence describes how a work may be shared or adapted. Readers should use the exact licence and version displayed on the article or its published PDF. Historical articles may carry different terms; a website redesign does not change those terms.

## Attribution and reuse
When reuse is permitted, identify the authors, article title and original publication, provide a link to the source and licence, and indicate changes where required. Do not suggest that the authors or journal endorse an adaptation. Check separately for third-party figures, photographs or other material excluded from the article’s licence.

## If a statement is missing
Free access is not a substitute for a reuse permission. Ask the editorial office to clarify the rights before redistributing or adapting material whose terms are uncertain. Include the article link and describe the proposed use.

## Authors preparing a submission
Review the publication agreement before accepting its terms. Resolve permissions for incorporated material before publication. Related guidance appears in [Copyright Policy](/policies/copyright) and [Open Access Policy](/policies/open-access).

# Corrections & Retractions

## Keeping the publication record accurate
Readers and authors should report significant errors promptly, identifying the article and explaining how the issue affects its results, interpretation or attribution. The editorial office assesses the evidence and seeks an author response where appropriate.

## Types of notice
A correction addresses an error that can be resolved without invalidating the central findings. An expression of concern alerts readers to a substantial unresolved issue. A retraction identifies work whose reliability or ethical basis is seriously compromised. The appropriate response depends on the evidence, not simply on who first reported the problem.

## Transparency
A publication notice should identify the affected article, explain the reason for the action and remain connected to its record. Corrections must not quietly replace substantive findings without an explanation. Legal or privacy restrictions may limit the detail that can be made public.

## Reporting and finding notices
Send concerns through the [ethics contact channel](/contact?category=ethics). Published notices can be found in the [Corrections / Retractions Archive](/corrections-retractions). General reference: [COPE retraction guidance](https://doi.org/10.24318/cope.2019.1.4).

# Complaints & Appeals

## Choose the appropriate route
An appeal challenges a particular editorial decision. A complaint concerns the handling of a submission, published material or the conduct of the journal. Research-integrity concerns can also be raised through the [ethics channel](/contact?category=ethics).

## What to include
Send the manuscript reference or article link, the decision or event you are questioning, a concise explanation and relevant supporting evidence. An appeal should identify a factual misunderstanding, an overlooked material point or a procedural concern. Repeating a disagreement without new reasoning is unlikely to resolve it.

## Review process
The editorial office identifies an appropriate person to assess the matter. Where the concern involves an editor, it should be referred for handling by someone not directly involved. Further information or an independent opinion may be requested. The response explains the outcome and any available next step.

## Professional communication
Keep correspondence factual and respectful. Do not contact reviewers directly or attempt to influence them. There is no guaranteed change of decision or fixed response time stated on this page. [Submit a complaint or appeal](/contact?category=complaints).

# Post-Publication Discussions / Comments

## Continue the scientific conversation
Readers can use the comment form on articles where discussion is enabled. Comments should address the article’s evidence, methods, interpretation or implications and give enough context for other readers to understand the point.

## Before you submit
Identify relevant competing interests. Refer to the passage, figure or finding you are discussing and include supporting references where useful. Do not include patient identifiers, private correspondence, defamatory allegations, advertising or unrelated personal information.

## Moderation
Comments are submitted for review and do not appear immediately. Moderation considers relevance, civility and substance. The name supplied with a guest comment may be shown publicly after approval; the email field is not displayed with the comment. Authors may respond through the same moderated process.

## Substantive concerns
A comment does not itself amend the publication record. If you believe findings are unreliable or an ethical issue requires investigation, use the [research-concern channel](/contact?category=ethics). Corrections and retractions follow the separate [publication-notice policy](/policies/corrections-retractions).

# Generative AI / AI Policy

## Human accountability
Authors remain responsible for the accuracy, originality and integrity of their submission, including material prepared with automated tools. An AI system cannot approve a manuscript, accept accountability or qualify as an author.

## Disclose substantive use
Describe the tool, its purpose and the part of the work it assisted with when it generated or materially revised text, code, analysis or other research content. Explain how outputs were checked. Verify every reference and factual assertion against the underlying evidence; do not submit invented citations or fabricated results.

## Images and data
Do not use generative tools to fabricate research images, observations or participant data. Any permitted illustrative or analytical use must be described accurately and must not misrepresent it as observed evidence.

## Confidentiality in review
Authors, reviewers and editors must not enter confidential manuscripts, personal data or unpublished results into external AI services without appropriate authorisation and safeguards. Reviewers must write and take responsibility for their assessment. A tool’s output is not a substitute for examining the manuscript and its evidence.

# Article Processing Charges (APC)

## Confirm the charge before committing
Publication costs should be clear before an author agrees to them. Request a written statement of the charge applicable to your article type, including currency, taxes, any additional services and the point at which payment becomes due. Do not interpret the absence of a displayed amount as a promise of free publication.

## What to ask
Provide the proposed article type and manuscript reference, if available. Ask whether the quoted amount includes all publication services and whether any optional services can be declined. Obtain the applicable cancellation and refund terms before paying.

## Editorial decisions
A payment or quotation does not constitute acceptance. Scientific assessment and the decision to publish must remain separate from financial discussions. Do not send payment to an individual reviewer or to an unverified account.

## Assistance
Authors who need financial support should request consideration before accepting an invoice; see [Waivers & Discounts](/policies/waivers). Use the [fees enquiry form](/contact?category=fees) for the current authorised quotation.

## Request support early

## Information to provide

## Editorial independence

# Archiving Policy

## Author self-archiving
Authors wishing to place a manuscript in an institutional or subject repository should check the publication agreement and the licence attached to the relevant version. A submitted manuscript, an accepted manuscript and the final published PDF may have different reuse conditions.

## Identify the version
Label the deposited file accurately and include the article title, authors, journal name and publication details. ## Restrictions and updates
Confirm any applicable embargo or third-party restriction before depositing a file. When an article is corrected or retracted, update the repository record or add a link to the notice rather than leaving readers unaware of the change.

## Clarification
If the agreement or licence does not clearly cover your intended deposit, [contact the editorial office](/contact). Website access and operational backups are addressed separately in [Digital Preservation](/policies/digital-preservation).

# ISSN / eISSN Information

## Understanding journal identifiers
An International Standard Serial Number identifies a continuing publication. Print and online editions may have different ISSNs. An ISSN identifies a title or edition; it is not a rating of research quality or evidence that a journal is indexed in a particular database.

## Verify before using an identifier
Use an ISSN only when it matches the journal title and medium in an authoritative record. Check the journal masthead and the [ISSN Portal](https://portal.issn.org/) rather than copying a number from an unrelated directory or a similarly named publication.

## Article identifiers
A DOI identifies an individual scholarly object and serves a different purpose from an ISSN. When citing an article, include the bibliographic details and DOI shown in its record, where available.

## Requests and corrections
If you need a confirmed print or electronic ISSN for a library, institutional form or submission record, [contact the editorial office](/contact). Report a suspected mismatch with a link to the record so it can be investigated.

# Indexing & Abstracting

## Verify the database record
Indexing describes a database’s inclusion of a journal or its content. Coverage can vary by year, article type and database. A journal title appearing in a search engine or on a repository page is not, by itself, evidence of inclusion in a selective indexing service.

## What counts as confirmation
Check the database’s own journal or source record and match the title, publisher and identifier. Where coverage dates are supplied, confirm that they include the period relevant to your article. A pending application does not establish acceptance.

## Claims and metrics
The journal should identify a service as verified only when its official record supports the claim. DOI registration, reader availability and individual article discoverability do not establish a journal impact factor, accreditation or database membership.

## Help with verification
Consult [Database Coverage](/policies/database-coverage) and ask the [editorial office](/contact) for an official record if you need confirmation for an institutional requirement. No guarantee of indexing an individual submission is made through this page.

# Abstracting / Database Coverage

## Coverage may differ between services
A database may list an entire journal, selected years or individual articles. Searching successfully for one article does not establish complete or continuing coverage of the title.

## Check a specific requirement
Before relying on a database claim for assessment, funding or promotion, confirm the official source record, coverage dates and your institution’s rules. Match the article’s DOI or full bibliographic details when checking whether a particular work has been included.

## Changes over time
Services can change their selection, metadata and coverage. Previously indexed material may remain discoverable even when new content is no longer added. Use the service’s current information rather than a historic badge or an unofficial list.

## Reporting a discrepancy
Send the article or journal link, database name and the record you checked to the [editorial office](/contact). Corrections to third-party records are subject to the database’s own procedures and cannot be guaranteed by the journal. See also [Indexing & Abstracting](/policies/indexing).

Use the author registration and sign-in pages. Enter accurate contact details and keep your password private. Maintain the name and affiliation you want the editorial office to use in correspondence.

Create a manuscript in the author workspace. Add its title, summary, article details and author information. Upload the required manuscript and any relevant supplementary material. Use filenames that make each document’s purpose clear.

# Article Types

## Match the format to the contribution
Select the article type that best describes the work. If the available submission options do not match your proposal, consult the editorial office before submitting.

## Research and evidence reviews
Original research reports a defined question, methods, results and interpretation. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses explain eligibility criteria, search methods, study selection and synthesis. Narrative reviews provide a balanced account of a topic and explain the basis for selecting the literature.

## Clinical and scholarly discussion
Case reports should explain the clinical learning point and include appropriate publication consent. Case series should describe how cases were identified. Editorials, perspectives and correspondence develop a focused argument or discuss published work, distinguishing opinion from evidence.

## Practical limits
Use the manuscript’s purpose to guide length and presentation, and remove unnecessary repetition. This page does not establish an unverified word, reference or figure limit. Ask the [editorial office](/contact) to confirm format-specific requirements, invitations or limits before preparation. Every article type remains subject to the relevant ethics and disclosure requirements.

# Manuscript Preparation Guidelines

## Title page and summary
Provide a specific title, accurate author names and affiliations, corresponding-author contact details and relevant declarations. Include an abstract and keywords appropriate to the article type. Avoid abbreviations in the title unless they are widely understood.

## Main text
Present the question, methods, findings and interpretation in a logical order. Explain participant selection, measures, analysis and limitations. Give units, define abbreviations at first use and distinguish prespecified analysis from later exploration. Do not present a statistical association as proof of causation.

## Tables, figures and files
Number tables and figures in the order cited, supply self-contained legends and check that labels remain readable. Upload research images without misleading alterations. Keep each image within the 5 MB upload limit and retain the original source files. Identify supplementary files clearly and refer to them in the text.

## Final checks
Remove patient identifiers and unnecessary personal information. Check citations against original sources, document permissions and include the required declarations. Follow any anonymisation instructions supplied for the manuscript’s review; do not assume the website removes identifying information automatically.

# Reference / Citation Style

## Make every source traceable
References should identify the actual source supporting a statement. Check author names, title, publication year, journal or publisher, volume and pages or article number against the original record. Include a DOI when available and verify that it resolves to the intended work.

## Consistent presentation
A numbered biomedical reference list is a suitable working format: number sources in the order first cited and use the same number when citing a source again. Keep formatting consistent throughout the manuscript. Follow any specific style instruction supplied by the editorial office during preparation.

## Information to include
For a journal article, provide authors, title, journal, year, volume, issue if applicable, pages or article number, and DOI. For a book, include the title, edition and publisher. For a webpage, name the responsible organisation, title, URL and access date where relevant. Identify datasets, software versions and preprints as such.

## Accuracy over volume
Cite relevant evidence rather than adding references to inflate counts. Do not invent references or cite an automated summary as though you had checked the underlying paper.

# Reviewers / Reviewer Guidelines

## Consider the invitation
Accept a review only when you have the expertise, time and independence to assess the manuscript. Declare relevant interests before accepting. If the deadline is unsuitable, contact the editor promptly rather than leaving the invitation unanswered.

## Read for substance
Assess the question, study design, ethics, methods, results and interpretation. Explain whether the evidence supports the conclusions and identify limitations that materially affect reliability. Check whether figures, tables and references allow the work to be understood. Do not require authors to make the paper resemble your own preferred study when its actual design is appropriate.

## Write a useful report
Separate major concerns from minor presentation points. Give specific, constructive reasons and distinguish essential changes from suggestions. Keep the recommendation consistent with the report. Put confidential concerns in the editor-only field, but do not hide scientific criticism there that authors need in order to respond.

## Submit through the reviewer workspace
Use the assigned record in the [reviewer dashboard](/reviewer/dashboard). Do not contact authors directly, share the manuscript or delegate the review without the editor’s permission. See [Reviewer Code of Conduct](/policies/reviewer-conduct).

# Reviewer Code of Conduct

## Be independent and respectful
Review the work on its merits. Disclose relationships or interests that may compromise impartiality, and decline when necessary. Criticism should address the research and its presentation, not the authors’ identity, nationality, career stage or personal circumstances.

## Protect unpublished work
Treat the manuscript, supporting data and correspondence as confidential. Do not use unpublished findings for personal advantage or share them with colleagues, students or external services without authorisation. If specialist assistance is needed, ask the handling editor first and disclose who contributed.

## Support your recommendations
Explain concerns with specific examples or evidence. Suggest citations only when they are substantively relevant; avoid requests intended to increase your own or another journal’s citation count. Report suspected ethical problems to the editor rather than investigating the authors independently.

## Communicate promptly
Tell the editor if circumstances prevent completion or if you discover a conflict after accepting. Submit an original assessment for which you take responsibility. Further guidance: [COPE ethical guidelines for peer reviewers](https://doi.org/10.24318/2019.1.4).
