## From submission to editorial decision
Peer review evaluates the relevance, methods, reporting and interpretation of a manuscript. Submission is not acceptance, and a favourable recommendation does not guarantee publication.

## Initial assessment
The editorial office checks that the manuscript, author information, declarations and supporting files are complete. The handling editor assesses scope, originality and ethical concerns. A paper may be returned for missing information or declined before external review when it is unsuitable for the journal.

## Specialist assessment
Suitable manuscripts are assigned to reviewers with relevant expertise. Reviewers must disclose competing interests, protect unpublished material and explain the evidence behind their recommendations. The editorial office should communicate the identity-disclosure arrangements applicable to the review before it begins; authors should not assume that identities are concealed unless instructed.

## Decisions and revisions
The editor may request changes, seek further advice, accept or reject the work. Revision requests identify the issues to address. Authors should submit a revised manuscript and a point-by-point response, explaining any requested change they have not made. Acceptance is followed by publication checks and proof preparation. An appeal must identify a specific error or procedural concern; see [Complaints & Appeals](/policies/complaints-appeals).
