## Participation and publication are different permissions
Authors must explain how consent for research participation was obtained or why an authorised waiver applied. A participant’s agreement to receive care or join a study does not automatically permit publication of identifiable details.

## Identifiable case material
Obtain appropriate publication consent before submitting a case report, photograph, recording or other material through which a person might be recognised. Explain to the person that published material can be accessible online and shared by others. Where a representative gives consent, describe the basis for that authority.

## Minimise identification
Remove names, record numbers and unnecessary personal details. A covered face or altered initials alone may not prevent identification. Do not change clinically relevant facts to disguise identity; omit unnecessary details and explain relevant de-identification measures instead.

## Records and enquiries
Keep the consent documentation securely and state in the manuscript that publication consent was obtained. Do not place signed forms in public supplementary files. If consent cannot be obtained, contact the editorial office before submission; publication must not be assumed to be permissible.
