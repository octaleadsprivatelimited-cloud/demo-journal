## Disclose interests that could influence the work
A competing interest may be financial, professional or personal. Disclosure allows readers and editors to assess the work with appropriate context; an interest does not automatically disqualify a contribution.

## Authors
Declare relevant employment, consulting, fees, grants, stock ownership, patents, paid testimony and support for the reported study. Also describe non-financial relationships or roles that could reasonably affect interpretation. Identify the funder’s role in design, analysis, writing and the decision to submit. If there are no relevant interests, state this explicitly rather than leaving the declaration blank.

## Reviewers and editors
Disclose close collaboration, institutional connections, personal relationships, direct competition or financial interests relevant to the manuscript. Do not accept an assignment when you cannot provide an impartial assessment. An editor with a relevant conflict should arrange independent handling.

## Updates
Notify the editorial office if an interest is discovered or changes during review. A material omission discovered after publication may require a published clarification. Questions about whether an interest is relevant should be raised before the editorial decision.
